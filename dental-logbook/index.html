Sound familiar?

Paper records fail in the same places.

None of this is about caring less. Binders in a busy dental office are easy to skip, easy to backfill and slow to search when someone asks for one record.

TodayThe binder

  • Load records filled in at the end of the day, in the same pen.
  • A failed spore test is written down, but nothing shows the retest or what was pulled.
  • Sterilizer logs, waterline results and OSHA training records in three different places.
  • When an auditor asks for one record, someone digs through a shelf.

PlannedDental Logbook

  • Each load recorded when it happens, with its mechanical and chemical indicators.
  • A failed biological indicator starts a guided retest, quarantine and recall checklist.
  • Sterilization, waterline and OSHA records in one place.
  • Any record found in seconds, then printed or exported for the person asking.

What we’re building

The checks you already do, in one record.

Written as plans, because they are plans. Nothing here is available yet, and every requirement will link to the guidance it comes from.

Sterilization

  • A register of every sterilizerIts type and the cycle types it runs, so every record points to the right machine.
  • Per-load recordsEach load with its mechanical and chemical indicators, recorded as the load is done.
  • Spore tests on scheduleWeekly in the United States. In Ontario, each day the sterilizer is used and each cycle type.
  • A guided response to a failed testA failed biological indicator starts a retest, quarantine and recall checklist, step by step.
  • Daily Bowie-Dick testsFor pre-vacuum sterilizers, as Ontario requires.
  • Implant loads heldAn implant load is held until its biological indicator passes.

Safety, water and equipment

  • Waterline test resultsRecorded against 500 CFU/mL for each dental unit.
  • Ultrasonic cleaner and washer checksOn a schedule you set, with the result and who checked.
  • Amalgam separator inspectionsInspections on a schedule, kept with the rest of your records.
  • OSHA recordsBloodborne pathogens and hazard communication: training, exposure control plan review and a safety data sheet index.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is the Dental Logbook available now?

No. It is in development. We are inviting dental offices to a free pilot, and pilot offices will be the first to use it.

Does it store patient information?

It is designed to store no patient information. It records sterilization and safety checks: machines, loads, tests and equipment. Nothing about patients.

Will it guarantee we pass an inspection?

No. No software can guarantee an inspection result. The Dental Logbook is being built around CDC, OSHA and EPA guidance and Ontario’s RCDSO standards, and every requirement will cite its source. Your regulator, your auditor and your infection-control program decide what your office must do.

What happens when a spore test fails?

Planned: the logbook starts a guided retest, quarantine and recall checklist. Each step is recorded as it happens, so the record is complete when someone asks for it.

How long are records kept?

Planned: records are kept at least 10 years. Corrections are added as new entries, so nothing is overwritten and the history stays honest.

Will this change how we work?

It starts from the records your office keeps now and the requirements you already follow. The point is to record them properly and find them quickly, not to add new work.

Which offices is it for?

Dental office managers and the staff who run the sterilizers, in the United States first, then Canada starting with Ontario. Elsewhere? Write to us and we will tell you when we are there.
