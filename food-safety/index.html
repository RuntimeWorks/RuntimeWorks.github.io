What the inspector sees

One report, ready when they walk in.

  • Every check with its time, reading, limit, result and the initials of whoever did it.
  • Out-of-range readings next to what was done about them, and the recheck.
  • Late and missed checks shown, not hidden. Saved entries can’t be edited; corrections are added as new entries.
  • Print it, or send it as a PDF. Every record can also be exported as a plain spreadsheet file.

Sound familiar?

Paper logs fail in the same four places.

None of this is about caring less. A clipboard on a busy shift is easy to skip, easy to backfill and hard to find later. That’s what inspectors notice.

BeforeThe clipboard

  • Readings filled in at the end of the shift, in the same pen, by one person.
  • Missing pages in the binder on inspection day.
  • A bad reading is written down, but nothing says what was done about it.
  • New staff don’t know which checks to do, or when.

AfterBoldsand logbook

  • Each check is timestamped and initialled by whoever did it, on their own phone.
  • Every record lives in one place, searchable, and prints as a clean report.
  • Out-of-range readings ask for the fix and a recheck before the check can close.
  • Reminders tell the shift what’s due, so nobody has to remember.

The rules, in brief

Built around the rules in each Atlantic province.

What each regulation says about records and day-to-day practice. Your inspector and your food safety plan decide what you check. Pilots start in Prince Edward Island.

  • Prince Edward IslandRecords: sanitizer test records kept 12 months (s.18). If your licence requires a food safety management system, its monitoring records are kept at the premises for 12 months (s.30).
  • Nova ScotiaRecords: if an inspector directs it, keep records that show your food safety system works, and show them when asked (s.37). Practice: keep food at safe temperatures, with an accurate thermometer in every fridge and hot-holding unit (s.33).
  • New BrunswickRecords: cold-holding temperatures taken and recorded at least twice a day (s.32(2)), hot-held food at least every 4 hours (s.32(3)). Class 5 premises keep process-control records at least one year, on the premises (s.20–21).
  • Newfoundland and LabradorPractice: processed hazardous food kept at 4 °C or colder or 60 °C or hotter (s.14), frozen food at −18 °C (s.15), accurate thermometers in cold rooms (s.10), utensils sanitized (s.26). The regulations set no general temperature-record rule.
  • Federal licence holdersRecords: documents that show your preventive control plan's monitoring, corrective actions and verification were carried out, kept two years (s.89). Traceability documents for each lot, one step back and forward (s.90).

Questions

Straight answers.

Do my staff need to install anything?

No. The logbook opens on any phone, tablet or computer. Staff tap a link, do the check and initial it. We show everyone how on day one.

What does it cost?

The setup session is free. For founding businesses, we agree on a monthly price with you before you pay anything, and that price stays the same as long as you stay. No long contract during the pilot.

Does this replace my food safety plan or my inspector?

No. Your plan and your inspector decide what needs to be checked. The logbook is where your team records that the checks were done, what the readings were, and what was done when something was off.

What happens to my records?

They belong to you. You can export or print all of them at any time, and if you leave, you take them with you. We only use them to run your logbook.

We already use paper and it works. Why change?

If your binder is complete, on time and easy to find, keep it. If you’d like to see the difference, a free setup session puts your own checks in the app so you can compare.

What if the Wi-Fi drops?

Checks still save on the phone or tablet, and they are sent as soon as the connection comes back. The screen shows when something is waiting to send.

Where are my records stored?

We are setting up the logbook to store pilot records in Canada, before the first pilot starts. They belong to you, and you can export all of them at any time.

Is it available in French?

Not yet. French is planned. If your team needs it, tell us and we’ll let you know when it’s ready.

Do you work outside PEI?

Not yet. Pilots start in Prince Edward Island. Elsewhere in Atlantic Canada? Leave your name and we’ll tell you when we’re there.
