## Who can ask, and how it starts

Each body asks under its own rule; here they are side by side.

The CDC plays no inspector role here. Its checklist is a self-assessment the office runs on itself: assessment of compliance should be conducted by direct observation of dental health care personnel during the performance of their duties <cite>(CDC infection prevention checklist for dental settings)</cite>.

## OSHA, the federal side

OSHA's power sits in its bloodborne pathogens standard: “The employer shall ensure that all records required to be maintained by this section shall be made available upon request to the Assistant Secretary and the Director for examination and copying.” <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard)</cite>

That clause reaches the standard's own records: the records required to be maintained by this section <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard)</cite>. The pack behind this page holds no OSHA power aimed at sterilizer logs as such, so this page claims none.

## What each state allows

Each state names who may ask, and sets its own limit.

### New York

New York's rule is about written requests: failing to respond within 30 days to written communications from the Education Department or the Department of Health, and to make available any relevant records with respect to an inquiry or complaint about the licensee's unprofessional conduct, is unprofessional conduct <cite>(New York rule 29.1(b)(13))</cite>.

The rules quoted here say nothing about inspection visits in New York; they hold this written-request duty <cite>(New York rule 29.1(b)(13))</cite>.

See our [New York dental sterilization rules](/states/new-york/).

### New Jersey

In New Jersey one enforcement statute covers every licensing board. Whenever it shall appear to any board, the director or the Attorney General that a person has engaged in an act or practice declared unlawful, it may “Inspect any premises from which a practice or activity subject to an act or regulation administered by the board is conducted” and “Examine any record, book, document, account or paper prepared or maintained by or for any professional or occupational licensee in the regular course of practicing such profession” <cite>(New Jersey statute 45:1-18)</cite>.

The dental chapter behind this page holds no inspection procedure of its own; our copy is the Division of Consumer Affairs' copy <cite>(New Jersey statute 45:1-18)</cite>.

See our [New Jersey dental sterilization rules](/states/new-jersey/).

### Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's inspection power is narrow on purpose. The board, through its authorized agents, may inspect a dental office with or without prior notice, for the purpose of determining whether the office is in compliance with the equipment and facility requirements for anesthesia permits <cite>(Pennsylvania rule 33.342)</cite>.

Those lists include “A sterilization area.” <cite>(Pennsylvania rule 33.342)</cite>, and the inspection is limited in scope by that section <cite>(Pennsylvania rule 33.342)</cite>. The chapter behind this page holds no other rule on sterilizer records <cite>(Pennsylvania rule 33.342)</cite>.

See our [Pennsylvania dental sterilization rules](/states/pennsylvania/).

### Florida

In Florida, duly authorized agents and employees of the department “shall have the power to inspect in a lawful manner at all reasonable hours” any establishment at which the services of a licensee authorized to prescribe controlled substances specified in chapter 893 are offered <cite>(Florida statute 456.069)</cite>.

Discipline stands behind the rule: failure to follow it “constitutes failure to provide reasonable sanitary facilities and conditions” and “constitutes negligence in the practice of dentistry” <cite>(Florida rule 64B5-25.001)</cite>.

See our [Florida dental sterilization rules](/states/florida/).

### Georgia

Georgia is complaint-driven. The board shall promptly conduct investigations to determine whether violations of the rules and laws governing the practice of dentistry or dental hygiene have occurred, and disciplinary proceedings may be initiated by the Board upon its own motion or upon receipt of a written complaint <cite>(Georgia board of dentistry rule 150-4-.01)</cite>.

How often? The board's infection-control committee minutes say the board office receives an average of 5 to 15 complaints concerning infection control a year <cite>(Georgia board of dentistry infection-control committee minutes, March 31, 2023)</cite>.

See our [Georgia dental sterilization rules](/states/georgia/).

## What to record

What each body may look at follows its enabling rule:

-   OSHA: the records required to be maintained by the bloodborne pathogens standard <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard)</cite>.
-   New York: any relevant records with respect to an inquiry or complaint about the licensee's unprofessional conduct <cite>(New York rule 29.1(b)(13))</cite>.
-   New Jersey: any record, book, document, account or paper prepared or maintained by or for a licensee in the regular course of practice <cite>(New Jersey statute 45:1-18)</cite>.
-   Pennsylvania: the office against the anesthesia-permit equipment and facility requirements, including a sterilization area <cite>(Pennsylvania rule 33.342)</cite>.

A good log answers these asks without reconstruction: kept current, naming what was processed and when, printing as it stands. What one sterilizer entry must hold is covered by our guide to [CDC's sterilization checklist items and the records that prove them](/guides/cdc-checklist-sterilization/).

Dental Logbook will keep these records in one place and export them on request; the judgment of compliance stays with the office.
