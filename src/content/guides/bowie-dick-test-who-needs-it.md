## Which sterilizers need the test

A Bowie-Dick test checks one thing: air removal. It is performed in an empty dynamic-air-removal steam sterilizer to ensure air removal <cite>(CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline)</cite>. Prevacuum sterilizers are the units tested for adequate air removal <cite>(CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines)</cite>.

A gravity-displacement sterilizer has no dynamic air-removal step for this test to check. If you are not sure which kind you own, the cycle name in your manual is the fastest tell: a prevacuum or vacuum-assisted cycle pulls air out before steam goes in.

## How often to run it

The two CDC texts differ on frequency, so here they are side by side.

-   2008 guideline: an air-removal test (Bowie-Dick Test) must be performed daily in an empty dynamic-air-removal sterilizer (e.g., prevacuum steam sterilizer) to ensure air removal <cite>(CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline)</cite>.
-   2003 dental guidelines: prevacuum sterilizers should be tested periodically for adequate air removal, as recommended by the manufacturer <cite>(CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines)</cite>.

CDC's dental monitoring page gives both texts as the places its sterilization-monitoring recommendations live: the 2008 guideline, and pages 24 to 25 of the 2003 dental guidelines <cite>(CDC, dental sterilization-monitoring page, 2024)</cite>.

Dental Logbook treats the test as due on each day of use, before the first load — the stricter of the two readings. In the logbook, the office records the test its own manufacturer specifies: a Bowie-Dick pack or a process challenge device.

## When else the test runs

The 2008 guideline also uses the test when a sterilizer is qualified: on installation, when the sterilizer is relocated or redesigned, after major repair, and after a sterilization failure has occurred <cite>(CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline)</cite>. At those events, three consecutive empty steam cycles are run with a biological and chemical indicator in an appropriate test package or tray, and each type of steam cycle used (e.g., vacuum-assisted, gravity) is tested separately <cite>(CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline)</cite>. In a prevacuum steam sterilizer, three consecutive empty cycles are also run with a Bowie-Dick test <cite>(CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline)</cite>.

Air-removal testing for prevacuum steam sterilizers is one part of a quality-control program for sterilized items, which also includes a sterilizer maintenance contract with records of service <cite>(CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline)</cite>.

## If the test fails

If a sterilizer fails the air removal test, it should not be used until inspected by sterilizer maintenance personnel and it passes the test <cite>(CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines)</cite>. In practice that is three moves:

1.  Stop using the sterilizer <cite>(CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines)</cite>.
2.  Have it inspected by sterilizer maintenance personnel <cite>(CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines)</cite>.
3.  Run the air-removal test again, and use the unit only once it passes <cite>(CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines)</cite>.

A good log keeps the failed test, the service record and the first passing retest together, so the gap in use is easy to explain later.

## What your state rules add

State boards reach this guidance in different ways. New Jersey, Pennsylvania and Georgia hold dentists to CDC's infection-control recommendations, so the air-removal guidance above reaches your office through those state rules <cite>(New Jersey rule 13:30-8.5; Pennsylvania rule 49 Pa. Code 33.211; Georgia rule 150-8-.01)</cite>.

Florida's dental sterilizer rule sets no air-removal schedule of its own; the 2008 CDC guideline is adopted there by reference for other parts of the rule, not for this one <cite>(Florida rules 64B5-25.002 and 64B5-25.003)</cite>. New York's rules do not address air-removal testing <cite>(New York rules of the Board of Regents, 8 NYCRR part 29)</cite>.

## What to record

A good air-removal record is one short entry per test:

-   date and time, entered before the first load of the day;
-   the test used — a Bowie-Dick pack or a process challenge device — and its lot;
-   pass or fail;
-   the operator's name or initials;
-   after a failure: the service call and the first passing retest.

Dental Logbook will ask for the day's air-removal test before the first load on each pre-vacuum unit, and keep that result beside the day's load records.
