Print one sheet per sterilizer and keep it by the sterilizer all year. Each week of 2027 has its own box for the spore test, the control from the same lot and implant-load tests — and the Florida sheet adds use time and the 30-day limit.

## The four rules this calendar is built on

Each rule below is quoted from the source that states it, with the row ID this site keeps it under. Nothing on this page adds a rule the sources do not hold.

-   Test each sterilizer at least weekly with a biological indicator and a matching control, meaning the biological indicator and the control come from the same lot number (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines).
    
    > “Monitor sterilizers at least weekly by using a biological indicator with a matching control (i.e., biological indicator and control from same lot number)”
    > 
    > <cite>CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines, Recommendation VI.F.6 · row C03-1</cite>
    
    > “A spore test should be used at least weekly to monitor sterilizers.”
    > 
    > <cite>CDC, 2016 summary of infection prevention practices · row SUM-1</cite>
    
    > “A biologic indicator (i.e., spore test) is used at least weekly and with every load containing implantable items”
    > 
    > <cite>CDC infection prevention checklist for dental settings · row CHK-1</cite>
    
-   Put a biological indicator in every sterilizer load that contains an implantable device, and verify the results before the implantable device is used, whenever possible (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines).
    
    > “Use a biological indicator for every sterilizer load that contains an implantable device. Verify results before using the implantable device, whenever possible”
    > 
    > <cite>CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines, Recommendation VI.F.7 · row C03-3</cite>
    
-   In Florida, steam under pressure (e.g. autoclave) and chemical vapor sterilizers must have their efficacy verified by appropriate biological monitoring at least once every 40 hours (2,400 minutes) of use or at least once every thirty days, whichever comes first (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    > “Adequacy of steam under pressure (e.g. autoclave) or chemical vapor sterilization must have their efficacy verified by appropriate biological monitoring at least once every 40 hours (2400 minutes) of use or at least once every thirty days, whichever comes first.”
    > 
    > <cite>Florida rule 64B5-25.003(9) · row FL-1</cite>
    
-   In Florida, dry heat and ethylene oxide sterilizers must have their efficacy verified with appropriate biological monitoring every 120 hours of operation at sterilization parameters or every thirty days, whichever comes first (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    Use time is determined by multiplying the number of cycles by the individual cycle time (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    > “Dry heat and ethylene oxide sterilizers must have their efficacy verified with appropriate biological monitoring every 120 hours of operation at sterilization parameters or every thirty days, whichever comes first. (Use time is determined by multiplying the number of cycles by the individual cycle time.)”
    > 
    > <cite>Florida rule 64B5-25.003(9) · row FL-2</cite>
    

## Where the sources differ — and where they are silent

CDC's recommendation and Florida's rule set different schedules. This page shows both and marks what each one does not say.

The sources on this page do not agree on one schedule: CDC's recommendation is at least weekly (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines; CDC, 2016 summary of infection prevention practices), and Florida's rule counts hours of use and days instead (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).

This calendar, and Dental Logbook, mark a test due at the earliest of the limits, so one sheet answers both schedules. That choice of “earliest” is ours; no source quoted here asks for a calendar at all.

### What the quoted sources do not say

-   They do not say which weekday a test must fall on. The boxes on these sheets are numbered weeks, Monday to Sunday; the office chooses the day.
-   They do not say what a calendar should look like. The layout, the columns and the box sizes are this page's design.
-   The Florida rows quoted here do not mention implant loads. The implant box on both sheets follows CDC's recommendation (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines).
-   They do not say how long to keep the filled sheets. This page sets no retention period.

## How to use it

Four steps, from blank sheets to a year of weekly tests on paper.

1.  ### One sheet per sterilizer
    
    Write the office name, the sterilizer's label and the year at the top. Print a second set for each extra sterilizer.
    
2.  ### Fill the week's box after each test
    
    Date tested, the BI product and its lot, the control from the same lot with its result, the result, and your initials.
    
3.  ### Record implant loads in the same week
    
    Every load that contains an implantable device gets its own biological indicator (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines). Write those tests in the implant box of that week's row.
    
4.  ### In Florida, keep the count too
    
    Add the week's cycles and cycle time, then the use-minutes this week, the use-minutes since the last test, and the days since the last test. A test is due at the earliest limit.
