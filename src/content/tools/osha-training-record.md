## What the rules say

-   Initial training is given when a worker is first assigned to tasks where occupational exposure may take place, and at least annually after that. <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030)</cite>
-   Annual training is given within one year of the previous training. <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030)</cite>
-   The record lists the date of each session and its contents, or a summary of them. <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030)</cite>
-   It lists the names and qualifications of the people who gave the training, and the names and job titles of everyone who attended. <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030)</cite>
-   Training records are kept for 3 years from the date the training took place. <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030)</cite>
-   On request, the records are made available to OSHA and NIOSH for examination and copying. <cite>(OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030)</cite>

## How to use it

1.  Fill in the header fields at the top of the log, then print the page on US Letter paper.
2.  For each session, write the training date, what was covered, and the trainer’s name and qualifications.
3.  Each attendee writes their name, job title and initials on their own line.
4.  Write the date the next training is due on the same line. It falls within one year of the session. Keep the finished record for 3 years from the date the training took place.
