## Written records, kept as you go.

What the sources say about sterilizer maintenance and the records around it.

-   Routine maintenance for sterilization equipment is performed according to the manufacturer's instructions, and it is documented by written maintenance records. <cite>(CDC, Infection Prevention Checklist for Dental Settings)</cite>
-   New York's infection-control rules name the same duty: maintain the equipment and devices used for sterilization according to the manufacturer's instructions. <cite>(New York Rules of the Board of Regents, 8 NYCRR 29.2)</cite>
-   A quality-control program for sterilized items includes a sterilizer maintenance contract with records of service. <cite>(CDC, 2008 guideline for disinfection and sterilization in health care facilities)</cite>
-   This log keeps one sheet per sterilizer: each visit gets its date, whether it was preventive work, a service call or a repair, and who did it, plus the next due date from the manufacturer’s maintenance interval.

## One sheet per sterilizer.

Fill it in on screen and print it, or print it blank and write on it. Keep the finished sheet with the unit's other records.

## Four steps, one sheet.

The sheet is a record of work already done on the unit, so fill it in as the work happens.

### Fill in the header

Office name, the sterilizer's label and the month, once at the top of the sheet.

### Log each visit

Date, the type of work, and the staff member or vendor who did it.

### Describe the work

What was done, any parts replaced, and the next due date from your manufacturer's maintenance interval.

### Sign and file it

Initial the entry, file the sheet with the unit's records, and print a new one for the next period.
