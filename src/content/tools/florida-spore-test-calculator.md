## The three limits, in the words of the rules

Every limit this calculator uses, first restated in plain words and then quoted exactly from the row of our source pack that holds it.

-   Each sterilizer is monitored at least weekly with a biological indicator and a matching control, a biological indicator and a control from the same lot number (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines).
    
    > “Monitor sterilizers at least weekly by using a biological indicator with a matching control (i.e., biological indicator and control from same lot number)”
    > 
    > <cite>CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines, row C03-1</cite>
    
-   Steam under pressure (e.g., autoclave) and chemical vapor sterilization must have their efficacy verified by appropriate biological monitoring at least once every 40 hours (2,400 minutes) of use or at least once every 30 days, whichever comes first (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    > “Adequacy of steam under pressure (e.g. autoclave) or chemical vapor sterilization must have their efficacy verified by appropriate biological monitoring at least once every 40 hours (2400 minutes) of use or at least once every thirty days, whichever comes first.”
    > 
    > <cite>Florida rule 64B5-25.003(9), row FL-1</cite>
    
-   Dry heat and ethylene oxide sterilizers must have their efficacy verified with appropriate biological monitoring every 120 hours of operation at sterilization parameters or every 30 days, whichever comes first, and use time is determined by multiplying the number of cycles by the individual cycle time (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    > “Dry heat and ethylene oxide sterilizers must have their efficacy verified with appropriate biological monitoring every 120 hours of operation at sterilization parameters or every thirty days, whichever comes first. (Use time is determined by multiplying the number of cycles by the individual cycle time.)”
    > 
    > <cite>Florida rule 64B5-25.003(9), row FL-2</cite>
    
    One hundred and twenty hours is 7,200 minutes, so this page counts dry heat and ethylene oxide in the same minutes as steam.
    
-   The rule names four kinds of sterilizer — steam under pressure (e.g., autoclave), dry heat, chemical vapor and ethylene oxide — and the devices used to achieve sterilization must be approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for sterilization (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    > “Steam under pressure (e.g., autoclave);” “Dry-heat;” “Chemical vapor;” “Ethylene oxide;” “Devices used to achieve sterilization must be approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for sterilization.”
    > 
    > <cite>Florida rule 64B5-25.003(1), row FL-4</cite>
    
-   The rule adds that it shall be reviewed, and if necessary, repealed, modified, or renewed through the rulemaking process five years from the effective date (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).
    
    > “This rule shall be reviewed, and if necessary, repealed, modified, or renewed through the rulemaking process five years from the effective date.”
    > 
    > <cite>Florida rule 64B5-25.003(11), row FL-8</cite>
    
    This page quotes the rule text that took effect on May 26, 2025 (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).

## How the calculation works

The formula, the rounding, and a worked example you can check by hand.

### The formula

Use-minutes a day = cycles a day × minutes a cycle (Florida rule 64B5-25.003: use time is determined by multiplying the number of cycles by the individual cycle time).

Days to the use limit = use limit ÷ use-minutes a day, rounded up to a whole day.

Due date = the earliest of the use-limit date, the 7-day date and the 30-day date (SOURCES.md, conflict row K2).

### Worked example: 8 cycles a day, 60 minutes a cycle

Use-minutes a day = 8 × 60 = 480 minutes.

Days to the use limit = 2,400 ÷ 480 = 5 days, so for a steam or chemical vapor sterilizer the 2,400-minute limit comes due on day 5 (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).

Say the last spore test was Oct 6, 2026. The use-limit date is Oct 11, 2026, the 7-day date is Oct 13, 2026 (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines) and the 30-day date is Nov 5, 2026 (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).

The earliest of the three is Oct 11, 2026 — day 5. At 8 cycles of 60 minutes a day, the use limit comes due before both of the others (Florida rule 64B5-25.003).

### Full-cycle counting

Florida's rule does not define the phrase ‘individual cycle time’ (SOURCES.md, conflict row K2).

This calculator counts the full logged cycle, start to end, so the minutes accrue fastest (SOURCES.md, conflict row K2).

## Where the rules disagree, and where they are silent

Two schedules can give two different dates. This page shows both and never picks a side quietly.

CDC's weekly schedule and Florida's schedule of use-minutes and 30 days can give different dates (SOURCES.md, conflict row K2).

This calculator shows all three dates and uses the earliest one (SOURCES.md, conflict row K2).

Whether a Florida office could switch off the weekly test and rely on the 40-hour rule alone is an open question we have not settled (SOURCES.md, open question Q2).

Until that question is settled, the 7-day limit stays in (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines).

The Florida rule text we hold does not define ‘individual cycle time’ (SOURCES.md, conflict row K2).
