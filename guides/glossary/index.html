Sterilization monitoring terms

Sterilization monitoring is the set of tests and readings that show whether a sterilizer did its job: a biological indicator (spore test) with its matching control, chemical indicators inside and outside each pack, the machine’s own readings, and, on pre-vacuum steam sterilizers, an air-removal test (CDC, 2003 dental and 2008 sterilization guidelines).

This glossary defines the twelve record terms in one line each, with the exact wording behind every line.

The terms, A to Z

Each line rewrites one quoted row and keeps its limits. Where the pack is silent, the line says so.

Air-removal (Bowie-Dick) test
How well a pre-vacuum steam sterilizer removes chamber air: an air-removal test (Bowie-Dick Test) must be performed daily in an empty dynamic-air-removal sterilizer (CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline). → The Bowie-Dick test: who needs it, and how often
Biological indicator
A spore test: monitor sterilizers at least weekly by using a biological indicator with a matching control (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines). → What is a spore test?
Chemical indicator, internal and external
Color-change checks that the agent reached the pack: place a chemical indicator on the inside of each package, and if the internal indicator is not visible from the outside, also place an exterior chemical indicator on the package (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines). → Chemical indicators: inside and outside the pack
Control
The unexposed partner proving the spores were alive: a control BI, from the same lot as the test indicator and not processed through the sterilizer, should be incubated with the test BI (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines). → Reading a spore test result
Cycle type
The cycle a load runs under, wrapped or unwrapped: when a sterilizer is qualified, each type of steam cycle used for sterilization (e.g., vacuum-assisted, gravity) is tested separately (CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline). → What a log entry must contain
Florida use time
Counted use hours in Florida: use time is determined by multiplying the number of cycles by the individual cycle time (Florida rule 64B5-25.003). Dry heat and ethylene oxide sterilizers must have their efficacy verified with appropriate biological monitoring every 120 hours of operation at sterilization parameters or every thirty days, whichever comes first (Florida rule 64B5-25.003). → Florida spore test due date
Implant load
A load that carries an implantable device: the items quarantined until BI results are known, though in an emergency this might be impossible (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines). → Implant loads and quarantine
Load number
The number that ties a pack to its run: packages should be labeled to show the sterilizer used, the cycle or load number, the date of sterilization, and, if applicable, the expiration date (CDC, Summary of Infection Prevention Practices in Dental Settings, 2016). → Labeling sterilized packs
Mechanical monitoring
Reading the sterilizer’s own gauges, displays or printouts. The rows quoted here are silent on this, so this is a product default, not a rule: a good load record checks temperature, pressure and exposure time against the manufacturer’s parameters. → Checking each load’s readings
Process challenge device
A test pack built to give the sterilizer a defined challenge. The rows behind this page do not define it; Dental Logbook lists it only as an air-removal test type. → The Bowie-Dick test: who needs it, and how often
Quarantine
Holding items back until a result is known: the items quarantined until BI results are known, though in an emergency this might be impossible (CDC, 2003 dental infection-control guidelines). → Implant loads and quarantine
Requalification
The series that puts a sterilizer back to work: biological and chemical indicators upon installation, relocation, redesign, major repair and after a sterilization failure, then three consecutive empty steam cycles with a biological and chemical indicator in an appropriate test package or tray (CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline). → Requalification after a repair or move

Where the source rows disagree

Three disagreements run through the source pack.

How often the air-removal test runs

Row C03-13 says “periodically … as recommended by the manufacturer”; row D08-4 says the test “must be performed daily in an empty dynamic-air-removal sterilizer” (CDC, 2003 dental and 2008 sterilization guidelines). Dental Logbook will run it on each day of use, before the first load.

Empty or loaded chamber when requalifying

Row D08-5 runs “three consecutive empty steam cycles”; row FAQ-6 re-challenges “with spore tests in three consecutive fully loaded chamber sterilization cycles” (CDC, 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline; CDC, sterilization monitoring page for dental settings, 2024). Dental Logbook records the chamber state of each cycle and does not choose for the office.

Weekly, or counted in use hours

Row C03-1 says “at least weekly”; row FL-2 for dry heat and ethylene oxide sterilizers says “every 120 hours of operation at sterilization parameters or every thirty days, whichever comes first” (CDC, 2003 dental guidelines; Florida rule 64B5-25.003). Dental Logbook will set the spore test due at the earliest of the dates these rows imply.

What to record

A good load record holds one row per cycle: date, start time, sterilizer, cycle type, load number, contents, wrapped or unwrapped, implantable device yes or no, temperature, pressure and exposure time checked against the manufacturer’s parameters, cycle minutes, indicator results, the operator’s initials, and the outcome — released, held for the spore test, or failed and reprocessed. A good spore test record adds the indicator’s lot and its control. No form here needs a patient’s name; the load number on the pack does the tying.
