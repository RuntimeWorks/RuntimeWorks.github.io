What the rules say

Written records, kept as you go.

What the sources say about sterilizer maintenance and the records around it.

  • Routine maintenance for sterilization equipment is performed according to the manufacturer's instructions, and it is documented by written maintenance records. (CDC, Infection Prevention Checklist for Dental Settings)
  • New York's infection-control rules name the same duty: maintain the equipment and devices used for sterilization according to the manufacturer's instructions. (New York Rules of the Board of Regents, 8 NYCRR 29.2)
  • A quality-control program for sterilized items includes a sterilizer maintenance contract with records of service. (CDC, 2008 guideline for disinfection and sterilization in health care facilities)
  • This log keeps one sheet per sterilizer: each visit gets its date, whether it was preventive work, a service call or a repair, and who did it, plus the next due date from the manufacturer’s maintenance interval.

How to use it

Four steps, one sheet.

The sheet is a record of work already done on the unit, so fill it in as the work happens.

Fill in the header

Office name, the sterilizer's label and the month, once at the top of the sheet.

Log each visit

Date, the type of work, and the staff member or vendor who did it.

Describe the work

What was done, any parts replaced, and the next due date from your manufacturer's maintenance interval.

Sign and file it

Initial the entry, file the sheet with the unit's records, and print a new one for the next period.
